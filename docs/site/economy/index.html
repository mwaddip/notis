Notis / economy

Two currencies, one rule.

Notis runs on karma — reputation you earn by contributing — and the notis — money that's mined, spent, and traded. Here's how each works, and the game theory that makes cheating cost more than it's worth.

The firewall

The single most important rule in the whole system: karma and notis never convert into each other. You can't buy karma with money, and you can't cash karma out for money — in either direction, the door is welded shut.

That firewall is what keeps reputation meaning something. If karma were purchasable, it would just be money wearing a costume, and the wealthiest — not the most valued — would look the most reputable. Sealing the two apart lets money be money and reputation be reputation, each doing its job honestly.

Karma — the reputation economy

What it's for

Karma is a legible, on-chain record of who the community values. The protocol doesn't try to judge quality itself — its job is to record endorsements honestly and keep the patterns visible, so people and apps can interpret them. The node keeps the books; the community reads them.

Where karma comes from — invitations with skin in the game

New karma is created in exactly one place: invitations. Only a member can send them, and only as many as they've earned the right to: the more the network has vouched for you, the larger your allowance, and every invite draws it down. Each one locks a bond of your own karma and grants the newcomer that same amount out of the shared pool; the bond flows back as they earn genuine likes, and forfeits if they never engage.

Why it's built this way

The scarce resource is endorsement, and it's the one thing you can't mint for yourself. A vouch only counts when it comes from a member older than you, so every endorsement that raises your allowance traces back through real members to the founders. A ring of accounts you control can vouch for each other endlessly and gain nothing — none of them carries that weight — so a fake account earns no allowance and invites no one.

The bond is a supply dial, not the defence: equal to the grant, it only sets how much of a completed invite's karma stays in circulation. Inviting real people is nearly free — the likes that vest your bond would have happened anyway — while a fake account never clears the first gate at all. Endorsement you can't fake, spent through an allowance you had to earn: that's the whole of it.

Likes — endorsements that have to cost something

A like isn't free. The liker spends a little karma; the author is paid most of it back, and a small sliver is burned. Why make liking cost anything at all? Because a free like is just noise — it carries no information. A like that cost the giver something is a real signal that they valued what they saw.

And because a fraction burns on every like, a ring of accounts liking each other's posts slowly bleeds karma instead of printing it. Genuine appreciation moves value toward good contributors; manufactured appreciation just drains the manufacturer. (It's all whole numbers — an author collects x−1 karma for every x likes, with one burned. No fractions anywhere, by design.)

Vouches — endorsing people, not posts

A like says "I valued this post." A vouch says "I stand behind this person." It's the one karma mechanic you can take back: you stake 1 karma on someone, and — unlike everything else on the ledger — that karma isn't spent or burned. It's held in escrow, still yours.

To withdraw, you unvouch, and your karma is released back to you after a cooldown. That delay is the anti-spam: you can't flip a vouch on and off to manufacture a signal, because pulling it out locks your stake for the cooldown before it returns. So a vouch is a cheap, reversible way to put a little of your reputation on the line for someone — without the permanence of a like.

Decay — reputation you have to keep earning

Karma isn't a trophy you win once. If an identity goes quiet, its karma decays; stay genuinely active and it doesn't. The hard part is stopping people from faking "active" on the cheap — so keeping your karma alive is tied to posting, and posting isn't free: a post pays a fixed price in karma to the shared pool, and nothing comes back.

The paradox that does the work

The only cheap way to keep your karma alive is to post things people actually like — the likes a good post earns are paid on top of its price. Wash-posting junk just to reset the clock pays that price over and over for nothing, which bleeds karma faster than the decay it's dodging. You cannot fake activity without paying for it, and paying for it costs more than sitting still would.

Sybil resistance, in one idea

You can't make a network like this truly un-gameable — given enough patience and accounts, anything can be Sybil-attacked. So the goal isn't perfection; it's to make gaming slow, visible, and more expensive than it's worth. The one door that mints karma — the invitation — opens only for members, and only as far as real endorsement has earned them, so a faker's reach is capped at what people who mean it will stake on them. And because every endorsement lives on-chain, unnatural accumulation leaves patterns that apps and indexers can flag. The protocol stays a neutral record-keeper — judgment stays with the community.

Notis — the money and security economy

What it's for

The notis is the utility layer: the money that pays for scarce network resources — storage, priority — and, above all, rewards the miners who secure the chain. The public square runs on karma; the notis runs the machinery underneath it.

Where notis comes from

New notis are mined through proof-of-work and issued as block rewards on a schedule that tapers toward a fixed cap — issuance slows over time instead of inflating forever.

Where a block's reward goes

A block pays out from three sources, and they are not treated alike. Newly issued notis are divided; fees are mostly passed through; storage rent goes entirely to the miner. Nothing here is a fixed percentage yet, but the order and the routes are settled.

Three things in that table are worth saying out loud. Karma operations pay no fee at all — posting, liking, inviting and vouching cost karma and proof-of-work, never money, or money would leak into the public square. Supporter shares draw only on newly issued notis, never on fees or rent, so they can never eat into what secures the chain day to day. And storage rent never reaches the reserve: rent is the perpetual security budget, so it goes to miners whole.

Within the coinbase the order is fixed: the reserve slice comes off first, then the miners' floor is guaranteed, then supporter shares take what's left, and anything they don't claim returns to miners. Unclaimed share doesn't accumulate anywhere — it is simply mined.

Paying for security — forever

A chain secured by mining has to keep paying its miners, or it gets cheap to attack. Once new issuance tapers off, miners keep earning from the two perpetual sources above: transaction fees, and storage rent.

Storage rent

A small charge on coins that sit completely untouched for years (about four) — it gently recycles genuinely lost or dormant coins back to miners. The effect is a permanent security budget without permanent inflation: nothing new is printed, long-lost coins simply return to circulation to keep the lights on. Coins you actually use never pay it, because touching them resets the clock.

A fair launch — no premine

Notis starts from zero supply. Nobody is handed a pile of coins at genesis, and there is no team allocation.

What exists instead is a claim on the flow. Early supporters can link their identity before launch and draw a share of the coinbase, proportional to what they held, for as long as they keep it staked and for at most two years. Those shares come from a pre-existing Notis token on Solana that funded development through creator fees — the project has never sold any of it.

A share of the flow can't be dumped on day one, isn't diluted by inflation, and can be exited whenever the holder chooses. Unstaking is one-way, so nobody can time it in and out. And the miners' floor guarantees miners keep a healthy share of every block no matter how many supporters take part, so the chain stays secure regardless of turnout. Miners receive notis only, keeping the social and value layers strictly separated.

The fine print

The reserve is locked under the current protocol — there is no rule that permits spending from it, so it accrues and sits. Unlocking it is a future protocol version's problem, not a key somebody holds. And notis carry eight decimal places — like most cryptocurrencies — handled as exact integers under the hood, with apps placing the decimal for display.

The through-line

If one idea ties it all together, it's this: value should be expensive to fake and cheap to earn honestly.

Minting karma takes an invitation, and invitations are rationed by endorsement you can't fake. Endorsing costs something, so endorsements mean something. Staying reputable costs genuine contribution. Securing the chain is paid for in perpetuity, so attacking it never pays off. And money and reputation sit in separate sealed compartments, so neither can corrupt the other.

Notis doesn't try to make cheating impossible — it makes cheating a losing trade.
